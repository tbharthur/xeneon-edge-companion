# Custom dashboard widgets

Updated: 2026-11-19.

EdgeCompanion has no plugin system; custom widgets were added to the local source and the app rebuilt/reinstalled. Its built-in web page widget can display a web dashboard, but the session reported only one such widget supported.

## Arthur's layout and design preference

Arthur requested the custom widgets on the main screen and no launcher/shortcuts. The installed layout is one page: the account quotas in a large (2×2) slot and the calls-and-sound card in the large slot beside it (since 2026-11-19; before that the call recorder and the monitor volume each had a tall slot there). Layout controls are in Settings → Dashboard → Widget layout. Earlier instructions to swipe to separate second/third pages are superseded.

Arthur found the first UI too small and the recorder too empty, so the custom widgets use large type and large touch targets. Final dashboard height was reduced to preserve the menu bar; see [split mode](split-mode.md).

## Account quotas

- Reads the same local quota feed and usable/blocked rules as Devboard, covering eight accounts: six Claude, then two Codex. This is broader than the app's original Claude Code/Codex usage widget.
- Shows 5-hour, weekly and Fable-weekly usage/reset information, plan and active-account indication. Usage thresholds are amber at 75% and red at 90%.
- The redesigned account tiles emphasize whichever usage limit is closest to exhaustion, with three small bars underneath. Blocked tiles turn red; Fable-blocked tiles turn amber. Both show the release time; the header counts ready/blocked accounts. A reset-time formatting fix preserves minutes for dates beyond today.
- Tiers (2026-11-03, Arthur): Claude Ultra plans (feed `plan` containing "Ultra"; currently gmail and arthur) are the top tier. They sort first among Claude accounts even when blocked and sit in a left column of larger tiles with a plan tag, a big number and labeled horizontal bars with percentages. The Team 5x seats, cascade and the Codex accounts form the second tier in a 3×2 grid of smaller tiles on the right. With only one tier present the original grid is used.
- Groups (2026-11-04, Arthur): the widget shows three labeled groups top to bottom: Claude Ultra as full-width rows (number left, labeled bars right), Claude Team as a tile grid, then Codex as a tile grid. Each group label carries its ready and blocked counts; the header keeps only the feed-offline pill. Grouping lives in EdgeCore `QuotaBucket`, with a test. This replaces the left-column tier split.
- One row per account (2026-11-11, Arthur): the widget was too crowded; what matters most is each account's email, whether it is blocked, and when a blocked account comes back. Each row shows a status dot, then the status on top ("Ready · N% used", "Blocked", or amber "Fable blocked" when only Fable is out) in the larger type, with the email under it at one fixed smaller size (Arthur, same day: the short status goes first so the email never has to shrink; colors unchanged). A blocked row's return sits at the right edge in the same shape on every row: the day, date and time in small red (amber for Fable) over a bold white countdown ("Fri 11/13 · 12 PM" over "in 2 days"; minutes are dropped on the hour; Arthur's layout, same day). The countdown uses minutes under an hour, hours under a day, then rounded days (EdgeCore `QuotaCountdown`, with a test) and refreshes with each one-minute poll. Times round to the nearest minute, so a 6:59:59 reset reads 7:00 PM. The per-window bars, big percentages and the active-account bolt are gone. Rows stay under small Claude Ultra / Claude Team / Codex labels; the header shows "N of M ready". Below about 420 points wide the status sits under the email. The status rule is EdgeCore `QuotaAccountView.headline`, with a test. This replaces the tile and labeled-bar designs above.
- Final row-layout verification limit (archived 2026-11-12): the status-above-email revision was reported built, installed and committed, but the Mac had locked at 4:26 PM and the dashboard is hidden while locked, so this final revision was not screenshot-verified on the panel. The longest email may not fit at the fixed size on blocked rows; the transcript ends before that concern is resolved. Earlier countdown and return-label revisions were screenshot-checked. The initial accounts/fader build reported all 73 tests passing; that does not establish a fresh test run for every later styling revision.
- Polls every minute while visible. If the feed fails, keeps the last values greyed out with a feed-status indication.
- Classification tests passed and small/large/wide layouts were rendered and refined. The redesigned preview used sample data with blocked-account examples while the Edge was asleep.

## Calls & sound card

One card (kind `callSound`, `CallSoundWidget.swift`), approved by Arthur on 2026-11-19, replaced the separate call recorder and monitor volume cards. It has no title. At Arthur's large slot (320 × 662, content 284 × 626) it is, top to bottom:

- Phone row: two 136 × 104 keys. Left: Phone (opens the Phone app), solid green Answer with a pulsing outline while ringing, On call on a green tint during a call. Right: Hang up dimmed with no call, solid red Decline while ringing, solid red Hang up during a call, amber Allow access when Accessibility isn't granted (tap opens the prompt).
- Recording panel (284 × 104): a Record key the size of a phone key, and a readout about the most recent recording. Idle: LAST CALL, finish time · duration, and the outcome (Sent to Discord / Saved, not sent / No audio captured / Recording failed / Didn't start); nothing without a previous run. Starting: spinner, "Getting the recorder ready…". Recording: red-tinted Stop key and panel, red dot + Recording, the timer in 38-point rounded digits. Stop still takes two taps within three seconds ("Tap again"). Transcribing: percentage ring in the key, call duration, "Then off to Discord" (blue tint). Sending: spinner, "Sending to Discord…". Recorder errors (e.g. "Recorder script not found") show in amber on the right.
- Sound section (the remaining 384): three equal channel strips 88 wide, 10 apart: Mic | LG | Sonos. The mic strip is one tall mute key (amber when muted, dimmed "No mic" without one). The LG (purple fill) and Sonos (blue fill) strips have the name and level on top, the fader, and a 72-tall Mute key, so all three mutes sit in one row along the bottom. Fader caps are 56 wide at this size.
- Geometry comes from EdgeCore `CallCardLayout` (tested): rows stay 104 in taller cards and shrink (to 56) in shorter ones; a card wider than 1.2× its height puts the sound beside the controls; a strip narrower than 64 moves the mic into a bar above the faders; a fader that would be under 40 tall is left out, leaving the level and Mute key; fader ticks are drawn only where the strip has room beside the cap. Nothing overflows its strip. Other slot sizes are a fallback only.
- Saved layouts: old `recorder` and `monitorVolume` slots decode as `callSound`. When a page had both, the card takes the first one's place and a slot covering both (EdgeCore `WidgetSize.covering`: two talls make a large). The layout model (`WidgetKind`, `WidgetSlot`, `DashboardPage`, cross-page de-duplication) lives in EdgeCore `DashboardLayout.swift` so the migration is tested: both cards on one page, one card, cards on different pages (the first wins), unknown and malformed slots, and loading twice. Arthur's saved page (quotas large, recorder tall, monitor volume tall) loads as quotas + the large card with no reset. The stored JSON keeps the old kinds until the settings are next saved; each load migrates again.
- The controllers and their behaviour are unchanged by the merge; each polls only while the card is on screen.
- Verified 2026-11-19: 84 EdgeCore tests pass; sample states (idle, ringing, on a call while recording with the mic muted, transcribing, starting, sending, LG muted/unreachable/waiting, Sonos unreachable, Local Network not allowed, no Accessibility + no mic + recorder error, and tall/wide/short fallbacks) were rendered with a throwaway offscreen harness (the app has no committed preview path). After install, the live panel showed quotas + the new card from Arthur's saved layout with no reset; the card measured 320 × 662 (content 284 × 626). No control on it was touched.

### Recorder

- Integrates the existing meeting-recorder script and pipeline (Settings: script path and recording name). The Poly Sync 20 speakerphone was permanently removed (2026-11-11): no output device is forced (the old `recorderOutputDevice` setting is ignored), so the recorder captures from the system default output. The recorder picks its own mic: Shure MV7 first, then the Insta360 webcam mic.
- Pipeline status writes for recording, transcribing, sending and done drive the states above. A real 9:46 test recording completed the pipeline and was reported sent to Discord (before the merge).

### Mic mute

- Mutes every physical microphone at the device level (CoreAudio input mute), so Teams, Zoom, Meet, phone calls and the recorder's mic channel all go silent. Virtual and aggregate inputs (such as Microsoft Teams Audio) are skipped. It reads Muted only when every physical mic is muted; one tap mutes all unless all are already muted. State is re-read every second. The caption names the mic an app has open (joined with "+" if several), falling back to the system default. On 2026-11-03 Teams was capturing from the Shure MV7 while another device was the system default, which is why it mutes every mic rather than only the default.
- Verified 2026-11-03: rule tests pass; the panel showed "Mic on · Shure MV7" while Teams held the MV7. The muted look with a real tap has not been exercised.

### Phone keys

- For iPhone calls taken on the Mac through the Phone app (Continuity). There is no public way to see or control these calls: CallKit's call observer is unavailable to AppKit apps, and the private call center (`TUCallCenter`) was rejected by `callservicesd` for lacking the "access-calls" entitlement. So the app reads the call windows through Accessibility, as VoiceOver does, and presses their own Answer, Decline and End buttons. The labels come from the system's own strings (FaceTimeNotificationUI and ConversationKit), in the Mac's languages plus English and Korean. Logic and tests: EdgeCore `PhoneCall`; reader: `PhoneController`.
- Fail-safe rules: a control is offered only when exactly one matching button is on screen. Ringing needs one Answer and one Decline in the same window. "Accept" is not treated as Answer, because calendar invitations offer Accept/Decline too. Anything ambiguous offers nothing to press. Every tap re-reads the screen and presses only if the call is still in the state the tap was meant for.
- Cost: macOS launches Phone when a call arrives, so nothing is read while Phone and the call-alert services are not running. While Phone runs, call windows are read once a second and Phone's main window (about 17-50 ms to read) every fifth second with its contact and recents lists skipped. Measured app CPU during a call was about 1-2%.
- Where calls appear: the ringing alert is a Notification Center custom alert drawn by `FaceTimeNotificationViewBridgeService` and `FaceTimeNotificationExtension`; if dragged it becomes a Phone window. The in-call window is a Phone window (`SceneWindow`, subrole `AXUnknown`) with an `End` button.
- Verified 2026-11-05 on a real outgoing call: the app found the in-call End button, showed On call with a red Hang up, and returned to idle after the call. **Unverified until a real incoming call:** that the Notification Center alert's Answer and Decline are readable with those labels, and that pressing them (and End) works. The app logs call-window button labels to `~/Library/Logs/EdgeCompanion/EdgeCompanion.log` (lines starting `phone:`) on every state change.

### LG monitor volume

- macOS has no software volume for the LG ULTRAWIDE's display audio, so the strip uses DDC/CI: MCCS 0x62 (volume, 0-100 on the LG) and 0x8D (mute: 1 muted, 2 unmuted), on the first external display that is not the Edge, and only those two codes (EdgeCore `MonitorAudioVCP`). The Edge stays limited to its colour codes. Controller: `MonitorVolumeController`.
- On the Edge a vertical finger drag arrives as scroll events and a tap as a click, so the fader (`Fader.swift`) uses an AppKit input view: drag follows the finger, a tap jumps to that point, momentum after lifting is ignored. Writes collapse to the newest value while the bus is busy.
- Reads run every 4 seconds while visible and pause for 2.5 seconds after a change, so the fader never jumps back mid-drag.
- The LG drops the first DDC request frame and answers an empty "not ready" reply, so monitor reads send the request twice, 10 ms apart (as m1ddc does), then wait 40 ms. Measured 2026-11-11: about 1 in 10 reads answered with one frame, 8-9 in 10 with two.
- A missed read is normal on the shared bus (MonitorControl, which Arthur keeps running, uses it too). The strip shows "Can't reach the monitor's volume" only after three missed volume reads in a row. A missed mute read keeps the last known state; a monitor that never reports mute is asked five times, then the strip tracks its own taps. MonitorControl's volume keys change the same volume; the strip picks that up on its next read.
- Running `m1ddc ... get` while the app polls returns garbage (for example 8224) because the replies collide; the app checks the VCP code in each reply, m1ddc does not.
- Verified 2026-11-11 against the LG (synthetic clicks and scrolls after a call): mute, unmute, tap-to-level and scroll drags all read back correctly. Not yet exercised with a finger on the panel.

### Sonos speaker

- Added 2026-11-19 at Arthur's request. Controlled directly on the local network through the speaker's own UPnP endpoints (RenderingControl on port 1400, the speaker's own Master volume 0-100 and mute, not its group's): no Sonos account, no Home Assistant, no credentials. Controller: `SonosVolumeController`; pure rules and parsing: EdgeCore `SonosAudio` (tested).
- Found by room name, never by address: Settings → Calls & sound widget → Sonos room (default "Office"). Bonjour (`_sonos._tcp`) lists the players through the system's mDNS, one of them (the wanted room's own first) is asked for the zone topology (`GetZoneGroupState`), and the room's visible player is used (a stereo pair is controlled through its visible half). After two missed reads the address is dropped and the speaker is looked for again every 10 seconds, so a new address or a speaker coming back from sleep recovers by itself.
- SSDP was tried first and rejected: the macOS application firewall (on, with stealth mode) blocks the unicast UDP replies to a self-signed app. Bonjour goes through mDNSResponder instead. The app's Info.plist declares `NSBonjourServices` (`_sonos._tcp`) and `NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription`; until macOS Local Network access is allowed the browse fails with PolicyDenied and the strip shows an amber "Allow Local Network in Settings" key that opens that settings pane.
- Reads every 3 seconds while visible; writes collapse to the newest value while one is in flight; reads pause 2.5 seconds after a change so the fader never snaps back. Unreachable shows calmly: the room name and "Asleep or away" in grey (with no room set: "Choose a Sonos room in Settings"). The rooms are re-read every minute while the speaker is found, which keeps Settings' "Rooms found" current and notices the room moving to another player.
- Safeguards: a read or search that comes back after the room setting changed is dropped; changing the room, losing the speaker or a refused Local Network clears unsent level/mute changes, so nothing stale is sent once it returns. Bonjour browsers and connections drop their handlers when cancelled, so repeated searches while the speaker is away don't pile up.
- Open after install (2026-11-19): the installed app's Bonjour browse is refused with PolicyDenied (Local Network not yet allowed for `pro.seol.EdgeCompanion`; no prompt was seen on screen), so the Sonos strip shows "Allow Local Network in Settings" until EdgeCompanion is switched on under Privacy & Security → Local Network.
- Verified 2026-11-19 through the app's own discovery and control code (run from a terminal process with network access): Bonjour found the household's rooms, the Office room resolved to its player, and a SetVolume of the volume just read was accepted and read back unchanged. No audible change was made. The household's room list and addresses are kept out of this public repository.
